# Four-Summary Thesis Plan: Ella

Complete this private editorial plan after the whole-dog profile and before writing any summary. Give each lens one distinct idea the reader should remember an hour later. The four theses must describe the same dog without becoming four paraphrases of one dominant motif.

For each lens boundary, state the unique reader question it answers, its one-sentence memorable answer, and at least one tempting chart motif or conclusion that belongs primarily in another lens. Shared facts may support several summaries, but the same motif may not serve as the remembered conclusion of more than one.

In the recurring-motif allocation, name the strongest motifs in the whole-dog profile and assign each a distinct editorial job: identity, daily life, present-tense support, development over time, supporting evidence only, or deliberately omitted. Do not solve four lenses by repeating one formula with different nouns.

The needs thesis explains present-tense support, regulation, handling, and enrichment. The growth thesis explains learning, development, challenges, and potential unfolding over time. State their distinction explicitly before drafting prose.

### Who the Dog Is — Identity Thesis

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.identity_thesis -->
Ella is an observant, socially invested original: she investigates independently, displays her participation, and treats return to trusted companions as part of the investigation rather than its opposite.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.identity_thesis -->

### How the Dog Lives — Daily-Life Thesis

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.daily_life_thesis -->
Ella's everyday life moves through phases of scanning, exploring, performing, and settling; clear transitions let her rich attention become rhythm instead of spillover.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.daily_life_thesis -->

### What the Dog Needs — Needs and Support Thesis

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.needs_support_thesis -->
Ella needs designed support—protected calm, fair boundaries, bounded choice, scent and task outlets, and relational acknowledgment—so safety and agency reinforce one another now.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.needs_support_thesis -->

### How the Dog Grows — Growth and Development Thesis

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.growth_development_thesis -->
Ella grows when the interval after surprise becomes more skillful: she can notice intensely, choose a practiced response, recover, and reconnect while keeping her distinctive spark.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.growth_development_thesis -->

### Who the Dog Is — Lens Boundary

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.identity_lens_boundary -->
Answers “Who is this dog?” and establishes enduring temperament, attachment style, and contradiction. Daily transitions belong primarily to How She Lives; concrete accommodations belong to What She Needs; learned recovery belongs to How She Grows.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.identity_lens_boundary -->

### How the Dog Lives — Lens Boundary

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.daily_life_lens_boundary -->
Answers “What does a day feel like from inside her pattern?” and focuses on sequence, arousal, activity, and closure. It should not become a general identity portrait or a prescriptive care inventory.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.daily_life_lens_boundary -->

### What the Dog Needs — Lens Boundary

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.needs_lens_boundary -->
Answers “What support makes life workable now?” and translates chart tensions into environmental design, enrichment, boundaries, and regulation. It does not claim that support equals development.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.needs_lens_boundary -->

### How the Dog Grows — Lens Boundary

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.growth_lens_boundary -->
Answers “What can become more available with time and practice?” and focuses on learning, recovery, reorientation, and potential. It must not demand that maturation erase temperament or substitute for present support.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.growth_lens_boundary -->

### Recurring Motif Allocation

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.recurring_motif_allocation -->
Investigation belongs primarily to identity as Ella's way of entering relationship; sequence and closure belong to daily life; den safety, bounded agency, and purposeful outlets belong to needs; novelty-to-return coordination belongs to growth. Visible pack participation supports identity and daily life. Scent and cue processing support daily life and growth. Bond-freedom tension is supporting evidence across the set, not a fifth thesis. No summary should make generic independence or routine its sole conclusion.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.recurring_motif_allocation -->

### Why Needs and Growth Are Different Arguments

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: summary_plan.needs_vs_growth_distinction -->
Needs are the conditions Ella requires from the environment and relationship today: a calm base, clear edges, choices, and legitimate work. Growth is what Ella can increasingly do within those conditions: recover sooner, reorient under manageable pressure, and carry curiosity without losing connection. Support builds the runway; development describes how she learns to use it.
<!-- END FIELD: summary_plan.needs_vs_growth_distinction -->
