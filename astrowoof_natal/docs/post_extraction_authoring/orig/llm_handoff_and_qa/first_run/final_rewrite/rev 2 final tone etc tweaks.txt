OVERALL EDITORIAL STANDARD

Write warm, observant, specific prose that sounds like a perceptive human who understands both dogs and astrology.

The cards should feel:

- affectionate without becoming sentimental
- intelligent without sounding academic
- playful without turning every sentence into a joke
- confident without presenting interpretations as clinical facts
- concrete enough to picture in ordinary dog life
- varied enough that the collection does not reveal a template

Prefer recognizable moments involving greetings, walks, thresholds, toys, meals, visitors, training, rest, search games, transitions, favorite people, household activity, recovery, and changes of plan.

Do not invent biography, events, diagnoses, breeds, medical conditions, or behavioral history not supported by the packet.

Avoid:

- “architecture”
- “projection” or “projected graph”
- “semantic system”
- “selected deck”
- “selected systems”
- “this synthesis rests on”
- “this claim combines”
- “the source material says”
- discussion of evidence assembly or authoring mechanics
- repetitive therapeutic language
- generic filler that could describe any dog
- exaggerated certainty
- repeatedly announcing the same interpretive formula

The evidence and derivation should inform the prose invisibly. End users should read an interpretation of Bre, not an explanation of how the artifact was constructed.

VOICE CONTRACTS

HANDLER

Address the human and describe Bre in the third person.

The handler voice should help the person recognize Bre’s tendencies, understand what may be happening, and respond constructively. It may offer gentle practical guidance, but it should not become a sequence of commands.

DIRECT_TO_DOG

Address Bre directly as “you.”

This must sound like an affectionate reading spoken to the dog, not handler prose with pronouns mechanically replaced. Keep it intelligible, warm, and respectful. Do not make Bre sound foolish or reduce every interpretation to treats.

HYBRID

Hybrid is a genuinely relational voice. It must describe an interaction between Bre and her person, not merely describe Bre to the handler with slightly altered wording.

Each hybrid body should contain, explicitly or implicitly:

1. something Bre contributes to the interaction;
2. something the person, environment, or relationship contributes;
3. the resulting rhythm, adjustment, feedback, or next possibility.

However, DO NOT force those elements into the same sentence template.

The previous “Bre brings X; you bring Y; her response tells you Z” construction may appear occasionally, but it must not dominate the collection.

Hybrid bodies should vary their organizing form. Use a mixture of:

- a shared activity or ritual;
- an observation about what happens between dog and person;
- an “if/then” interaction;
- complementary roles expressed naturally;
- a moment of mutual adjustment;
- a progression across two or three beats;
- a relationship outcome that develops over time;
- an invitation to notice Bre’s response;
- occasional direct “Bre brings / you bring” language.

Do not begin more than 20% of hybrid bodies at any density with “Bre” or “Bre’s.”

Do not begin more than 20% with a stock transition such as:

- “In practice”
- “Here”
- “At this point”
- “As the interaction unfolds”
- “Within this exchange”

Across all 50 cards, do not repeatedly end with:

- “Her response tells you…”
- “Her next move shows…”
- “Her return tells you…”
- “This tells you whether…”

Preserve the useful feedback idea, but express it in varied ways or leave it implicit when the interaction already demonstrates it.

Hybrid should not always cast the human as the rule-giver and Bre as the reactor. Depending on the claim, Bre may initiate, invite, interrupt, investigate, regulate the pace, make a preference visible, restore play, request distance, or lead the pair toward useful information. The human may support, notice, answer, protect space, join, redirect, clarify, or simply refrain from crowding the moment.

A good hybrid card should be identifiable as hybrid even if the labels are removed.

ASTROLOGY DENSITY

NO_ASTRO

Use no explicit astrological terminology. Translate the claim completely into recognizable behavior, temperament, relationship, and dog-life situations.

LIGHT_ASTRO

Name the most useful placement, aspect, or configuration, then move promptly into interpretation. Do not overload the body with technical explanation.

FULL_ASTRO

Explain the relevant astrological components and how they combine, but still write for a reader rather than a graph-processing system.

Projected registry vocabulary may be used when it adds flavor or precision, especially established AstroWoof terms such as Doghouse. Decode registry meanings accurately. Do not dump registry definitions, mechanically concatenate compound phrases, or force unusual projected vocabulary into every card.

The astrology-density setting governs the body and headline’s astrological explicitness. It does not make the dog quote, do list, or don’t list progressively more technical. Dog quotes should remain doglike at every density.

HEADLINES

Make headlines concise, memorable, and meaningfully differentiated among handler, direct-to-dog, and hybrid.

Avoid mechanically repeating the same astrological noun plus a generic verb. Hybrid headlines should suggest cooperation, rhythm, exchange, or a shared situation without making every headline begin with “Together,” “Let,” or “Build.”

DOS AND DON’TS

Make these concrete and usable.

Each item should follow naturally from its claim rather than repeat generic advice such as “be patient,” “stay consistent,” or “give space” without situational detail.

Do not convert an interpretive tendency into a rigid behavioral prescription. Vary sentence structures and avoid repeating the same advice across neighboring cards unless the context materially differs.

DOG QUOTES AND JOKES

Keep one stable set of dog-voice humor per claim across astrology-density variants unless the schema explicitly requires otherwise.

Dog quotes should be short, distinctive, and tied to the claim’s actual behavioral image. They should sound like different observations from the same dog—not 50 versions of “I require snacks.”

Humor should add personality after the card already makes sense. It must not carry essential interpretive content.
