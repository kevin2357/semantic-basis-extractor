# Phase 3: Ella Compact Full-Chart Live A/B

## Verdict

The compact full-chart transport passed its first live quality checkpoint.
Against the preserved Ella legacy pass-6 control, the compact treatment retained
the same whole-dog understanding, produced four clearly separated summary
arguments, created valid independent chapter plans, passed opaque pass QA on
its first attempt, and completed final structural validation.

The treatment did not show a material quality loss. It was somewhat tighter
and more canonically explicit; the control retained a few marginally stronger
individual literary flourishes. Both are publishable-quality baselines. This is
the result needed to continue testing, not yet enough evidence to make compact
transport the production default.

## Experimental design

Only pass 6 was newly authored. Passes 1–5 were copied byte-for-byte from the
same preserved Ella baseline used by the earlier structured-chapter test.

- **Control:** the accepted legacy-basis Ella pass 6 and final deck from
  `phase-003-live-ella-chapters`.
- **Treatment:** a fresh pass 6 generated with
  `--full-chart-basis-format compact-v1`.
- **Model:** `gpt-5.6-terra`.
- **Reasoning:** medium.
- **Attempts allowed:** three.
- **Attempts used:** one.
- **Polish:** disabled.
- **Pass assignment:** contiguous in both runs; irrelevant to summary-only pass
  6 but held constant.
- **Known incidental wording change:** the shared authoring brief calls the
  projected definitions a “decoder” rather than a “registry,” so it accurately
  describes both transports.

The compact JSON traceability sidecar remained local. It was not included in
the authoring ZIP or API prompt.

## Service result and cost

The compact treatment completed in 122.9 seconds and passed opaque QA on
attempt 1.

| Metric | Legacy control, first request | Compact treatment |
|---|---:|---:|
| Input tokens | 44,451 | 36,841 |
| Output tokens | 8,231 | 8,089 |
| Estimated request cost | $0.2346 | $0.2134 |
| Result | Rejected for chapter balance | Accepted |

The 17.1% actual input-token reduction is smaller than the 37.5% subject-prefix
reduction because the static instructions, summary assignment, gold reference,
and response schema were unchanged. This one-call isolation had no cache hit.

The legacy control required a second request to repair chapter balance. Its
accepted second attempt used 44,625 input tokens, of which 26,741 were cached,
plus 8,713 output tokens, at an estimated $0.1821. Total control experiment cost
was therefore approximately $0.4167 versus $0.2134 for this treatment. That
retry difference is useful pipeline evidence but cannot be attributed solely
to compact transport.

Matched no-service six-pass layouts provide the better estimate of recurring
transport effects:

- subject prefix: 19,416 → 12,138 estimated tokens;
- aggregate six-request input: 381,673 → 338,005 estimated tokens;
- reduction: 37.5% in the cached subject tier and 11.4% gross across requests.

Actual future six-pass billing should benefit from the reduced subject prefix
at one uncached/cache-write rate and five cached-input rates.

## Whole-dog comprehension

Both profiles found the same organizing portrait:

- alert, detailed investigation;
- visible participation and useful pack role;
- serious one-to-one trust;
- outward activity balanced by a genuine private reset;
- learning through clear signals, structure, and cooperative practice;
- development through the sequence after surprise.

The control compresses this as “a bright, serious little participant” who wants
her findings to matter. The treatment calls her “a small, serious scout” whose
careful investigation seeks “an acknowledged place in the visible pack.” These
are compatible readings, not divergent dogs.

The treatment profile was longer—899 words versus 795—despite receiving the
shorter source transport. That is strong evidence against the concern that
compaction would automatically produce a thinner mental model. It also made
several source relationships more explicit:

- Scorpio Ascendant with first-Doghouse Saturn;
- Virgo Sun/Jupiter/Mercury;
- Leo Mars/Venus/MC;
- exact ASC–DSC and IC–MC axes;
- Moon–Uranus friction;
- Mercury–Saturn and Mercury/Node developmental channels.

The control is slightly more editorially compressed. The treatment is slightly
more useful as an auditable astrological orientation. Neither invents biography
or diagnosis.

## Four-summary separation

Both thesis plans independently converged on four clean arguments:

1. **Identity:** a discerning investigator who wants a recognized place with
   her people.
2. **Daily life:** outward patrol and participation need an unmistakable return
   to home-based quiet.
3. **Needs:** dependable boundaries, protected recovery, bounded choices, and
   useful outlets.
4. **Growth:** practice should turn novelty into orienting, choosing,
   cooperating, and recovering.

Both explicitly distinguish present-tense support from developmental capacity.
The compact treatment therefore retained the central full-chart synthesis that
pass 6 exists to produce.

The treatment is 9.5% shorter overall. It is not compressed into blurbs: the

but summary-length policy remains a product decision rather than an automatic
consequence of this test.

## Writing-quality comparison

### Where the treatment is especially strong

- It opens `Who She Is` with “every room has a job description, even if nobody
  has printed it yet,” a concrete behavioral doorway rather than an abstract
  trait inventory.
- `How She Lives` uses the organizing image of a day with chapters and an
  “inner clipboard,” giving activity and recovery one memorable structure.
- `What She Needs` produces the useful hybrid headline “The House Says Yes,
  No, and Here,” a concise description of bounded permission.
- `How She Grows` preserves Ella's intensity rather than treating maturity as
  personality removal: “Keep the Spark; Practice the Return.”
- Full-astrology bodies are orderly and selective. They name the primary
  placements and geometries, explain their joint meaning, and return to usable
  handling.

### Where the control retains an edge

The legacy control contains several unusually memorable turns:

- “curiosity has legal outlets”;
- “The Boundary With a Door in It”;
- “Keep the Nose; Add a Radio”;
- “Rest does not cancel the patrol.”

The treatment has comparable but not uniformly superior literary peaks. This
is normal response variance and is a reason to judge the transport across more
than one subject. There is no sign that the compact representation forbids
high-quality imagery; it simply did not win every line-level comparison.

### Voice and density

The three audiences remain functionally distinct:

- handler mode explains and advises;
- direct-to-dog mode reassures Ella without infantilizing her;
- hybrid mode stages the reciprocal ritual between Ella and her person.

Density remains progressive disclosure. No-astrology prose stands on behavior,
light astrology introduces selected source concepts, and full astrology adds
geometry and interacting placements. Projected source machinery does not leak
into reader-facing prose.

## Chapter plans

The treatment produced five Interdogpendence chapters and four Takeaways
chapters. The two systems are visibly and semantically independent.

Interdogpendence organizes relationship dynamics:

- When Drive Becomes a Public Force;
- Partnership Across the Leash;
- Private Refuge, Visible Role;
- Attention Shaped Into Skill;
- Surprise, Intensity, and Adjustment.

Takeaways organizes integrated conclusions:

- The Skills That Become Easier Together;
- Making Big Energy Usable;
- A Social Dog With a Private Reset;
- Exploration That Keeps the Bond in View.

The titles fit the AstroWoof visual language, include relevant emoji, satisfy
balance rules, and avoid mirrored taxonomies. The treatment passed the pass-6
gate on its first attempt, unlike the legacy control's first attempt.

## QA interpretation

Final validation passed with four advisory warnings; two refer to inherited
card passes and two to summary phrases that the lexical astrology detector may
be over-reading. The whole-deck linter produced one warning about the
“fine-print” mechanism, entirely in inherited ordinary cards. No treatment
summary caused that warning.

Thus the compact pass added no deterministic editorial-lint defect. The final
deck reached `DELIVERY_COMPLETE_WITH_WARNINGS`, matching ordinary accepted
pipeline behavior.

## Decision

Keep `compact-v1` experimental and proceed to a second-subject matched test.
The first live result supports all of the following:

- compact transport is intelligible to a naive authoring response;
- it preserves whole-dog synthesis and evidence-bounded astrology;
- it can support rich prose rather than forcing compression;
- it materially reduces input tokens;
- its structured chart map may improve source visibility;
- it does not interfere with chapter planning or opaque QA.

Do not make it the default from one stochastic comparison. The next subject
should use two fresh pass-6 requests—legacy and compact—generated from the same
current code and source packet, avoiding same-subject summary gold. If that test
also shows parity, compact transport has enough evidence for default-candidate
status while the SPC canonical capsule remains a separate upstream enhancement.
