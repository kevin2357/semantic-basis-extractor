# Whole-Dog Authoring Profile: Ella

Complete this profile first, using `DOG DETAILS.md` and the entire `FULL CHART BASIS.md`. This becomes the shared characterization for every story in the workspace.

### Integrated Portrait

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.integrated_portrait -->
Ella is a compact, alert Miniature Schnauzer whose life seems organized around two simultaneous convictions: the world contains important information, and her people are worth returning to. She investigates smells, sounds, changes, and household activity with serious attention, often treating ordinary events as assignments. Yet her curiosity is not rootless. A chosen companion, a familiar resting place, or a clear social rhythm gives her a dependable center. She can be watchful, affectionate, funny, intense, and unexpectedly unconventional without becoming incoherent: Ella is a dog who turns sensitivity into participation when the environment offers structure and relationship.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.integrated_portrait -->

### Core Temperament

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.core_temperament -->
Her temperament is observant, purposeful, and quick to engage. She notices small changes and may respond immediately, especially when novelty appears inside a familiar routine. She also has a pronounced pack orientation: she wants to know what is happening, where everyone is, and what useful role she can play. Her confidence often grows through inspection, contribution, and visible participation rather than passive exposure. Under the alertness is a practical loyalty that prefers chosen people, familiar rituals, and tangible signs of belonging.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.core_temperament -->

### Emotional Life and Regulation

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.emotional_regulation -->
Ella regulates through a combination of proximity, private retreat, sensory processing, and patterned activity. A favored resting place or unusual nook may help her settle, even when the arrangement looks eccentric to humans. Comfort and novelty can pull against each other: a surprise may interrupt rest and propel immediate action. Recovery is supported when she can investigate safely, connect with a trusted person, use a known outlet, and return to a predictable calming ritual. Her odd comfort preferences should be judged by whether they help her settle, not by whether they look conventional.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.emotional_regulation -->

### Learning and Motivation

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.learning_motivation -->
Ella learns best when attention has a job. Scent work, small investigations, clear sequences, and repeatable cues give her perceptive mind something constructive to do. She can develop a pause between noticing and acting when practice is gradual and the alternative behavior has a satisfying outlet. Rules are most motivating when they organize information rather than merely restrict her. Social learning is equally important: waiting, taking turns, settling near the group, and returning to shared activity are meaningful growth skills, not just obedience exercises.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.learning_motivation -->

### Relationships and Trust

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.relationships_trust -->
Ella forms close, selective bonds and expresses loyalty through dependable proximity, return, and shared quiet. Her favorite companion may function as a secure reference point during investigation or novelty. Trust does not require constant contact; it allows her to move outward, gather information, and come back. A respectful handler gives her room to assess, keeps cues consistent, protects her retreat, and makes reunion rewarding. She is likely to experience relationship as active coordination: the human provides meaning and boundaries while Ella contributes attention, humor, vigilance, and faithful participation.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.relationships_trust -->

### Play, Adventure, and Daily Rhythm

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.play_adventure_rhythm -->
Daily life may alternate between patrol, play, unusual rest, and renewed participation. Ella enjoys exploration when it feels connected to the pack and can turn a walk into a detailed inspection. Novelty can produce an immediate chase or zoom response, but that same energy can become a search, tug game, recall exercise, or shared adventure. Her best rhythm includes both expansion and return: safe opportunities to investigate, followed by familiar cues and a route back to comfort. She is not simply high-energy or simply home-oriented; she is a dog whose movement gains meaning through the cycle of departure and reconnection.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.play_adventure_rhythm -->

### Tensions and Counterweights

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.tensions_counterweights -->
Several counterweights shape Ella. Investigation faces off with settled companionship; private, unconventional safety faces visible pack participation; comfort can conflict with a quick novelty response; and a strong desire to inspect can meet the need for rules. These are not diagnoses or fixed contradictions. They are recurring negotiations. Ella does well when neither pole is humiliated: curiosity gets a safe boundary, retreat remains available, public participation has a manageable role, and training converts activation into a sequence. Her bond is a return address, not a permanent tether.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.tensions_counterweights -->

### Strengths and Growth Edges

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.strengths_growth -->
Her strengths include close observation, scent and cue sensitivity, persistence, loyalty, social awareness, willingness to participate, and the ability to make practice meaningful. She can become especially capable when sensation, relationship, and structure are linked. Growth edges include pausing before immediate pursuit, tolerating small changes inside routine, sharing attention, and transitioning smoothly between private reset and public role. These should be developed through brief, fair repetitions and useful outlets. The aim is not to flatten her intensity or eccentricity, but to help her choose where that energy lands.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.strengths_growth -->

### Voice, Warmth, Humor, and Imagery

<!-- BEGIN FIELD: profile.voice_humor_imagery -->
Write Ella with warmth, precision, and affectionate respect. She can be imagined as a miniature patrol supervisor, scent detective, household committee member, or employee with an unusually designed private office, but the humor should arise from her exact priorities: the seriousness of a new smell, the official importance of a nap spot, the instant promotion of a sock to emergency status. Keep her dignity intact. Favor concrete images—doorway pauses, check-ins, chosen corners, perimeter surveys, return cues, and shared settling—over generic claims about dogs.
<!-- END FIELD: profile.voice_humor_imagery -->
